# The Learning Dispatch: A Year in Review

*Year-end retrospectives are a minor genre unto themselves, and I am not entirely sure what purpose they serve beyond the ritual marking of time. But looking back at what I wrote this year on Substack, certain preoccupations become visible that were not always apparent in the writing of them: the defence of evidence over intuition, the critique of ideas that have drifted too far from their origins, the emergence of AI as both tutor and thief, and a persistent concern with what happens to the mind when we stop asking it to do difficult things.  *

*The Learning Dispatch began on the 13th of February this year with the intent of writing quick responses to new research I was reading and trying to make sense of it for myself as much as anyone else. I also wanted a space to write longer pieces on the broader culture of education: its fashions, blind spots and the counterintuitive truths about learning that close scrutiny reveals. Finally, I wanted to publish a monthly round-up of new research for anyone who wants to learn more about learning but doesn’t know where to start. *

*This then is less a year in review than ten months of finding my footing. So here are the top ten most-read posts in order that resonated with readers. I am grateful to everyone who has subscribed; it is a strange and heartening thing to write into the void and then discover that someone is listening. Thank you. *
